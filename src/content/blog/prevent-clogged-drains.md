Clogged drains are one of the most common plumbing calls. Many of them build up slowly from everyday habits. A few consistent practices can reduce how often you need professional clearing.

## What Usually Causes Clogs

In kitchens, grease and food particles are the main culprits. Even with a disposal, oils can coat pipe walls and trap other debris. In bathrooms, hair, soap scum, and products that do not dissolve fully create restrictions over time.

Main lines can also collect roots or foreign objects, but household prevention still helps the fixtures you use every day.

## Habits That Help

**Kitchen**

- Wipe grease into the trash instead of rinsing it down the sink.
- Use a simple strainer to catch food scraps.
- Run cold water when using the disposal, and avoid fibrous or starchy waste when possible.

**Bathroom**

- Use a drain hair catcher in showers and tubs.
- Avoid flushing wipes, even those labeled flushable—they often do not break down like toilet paper.
- Limit how much hair and product residue goes down the drain.

## When to Call for Help

If multiple fixtures drain slowly, or you hear gurgling, the issue may be farther down the line. Persistent problems after basic clearing are a good time to schedule professional service. Early attention can prevent a full backup.

Regular care will not eliminate every clog, but it meaningfully reduces how often drains become a crisis.
