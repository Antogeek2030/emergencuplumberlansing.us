Water heaters work hard and often out of sight. A little attention can extend useful life and reduce the chance of sudden failure—especially in colder months when demand is high.

## For Tank Water Heaters

Sediment buildup is common in areas with hard water. It reduces efficiency and can stress the tank. Periodic flushing, following the manufacturer’s guidance, helps. If you are not comfortable with the process, a service visit can include inspection and flushing.

Check the temperature setting. Many homes are comfortable around 120°F. Higher settings increase energy use and scald risk; lower settings may not satisfy demand or can encourage bacterial growth in some situations.

Look at the area around the tank for moisture or rust. Early signs of leakage are easier to address than a full failure.

## For Tankless Units

Tankless heaters benefit from descaling on a schedule suited to your water hardness. Inlet filters should be checked and cleaned as recommended. Error codes are there to help—note them before resetting so a technician can diagnose accurately if needed.

## When to Consider Replacement

Age, repair frequency, and rising energy use all factor into the decision. If your unit is past typical lifespan and needs another major part, comparing repair cost to a new, more efficient model is worthwhile. A professional can help weigh options for your home and budget.

Routine attention will not make a water heater last forever, but it improves reliability and gives you more control over when replacement happens.
